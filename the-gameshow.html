The Gameshow

The Live Show the
Whole Room Plays

Ninety minutes, three rounds, and a host who can hold a room. Built on the TV formats your guests grew up with, and run for groups of any size, whether you are together in one room or spread across screens.

What It Is

Most event entertainment asks people to opt in. A speaker hopes you will listen. A band hopes you will dance. We built something nobody has to be talked into.

The Gameshow is exactly what it sounds like: a live, hosted show run across three rounds, modelled on formats the whole room already understands. There are a few rules to explain, but they are simple ones, and no warm-up that makes grown adults wince. The host says go, and people go.

It works because the format does the heavy lifting. You are not relying on your team to "be up for it". The quiet ones compete just as hard as the loud ones, because the structure gives them cover to.

The Rounds

Three Rounds Your Guests Can Play in Their Sleep

Each one takes its cue from a format the room will recognise on sight. They are our own builds, inspired by Family Fortunes/Family Feud, The Cube and Countdown, with a modern twist.

Round 01

Family Fortunes/Family Feud

We surveyed; your teams guess. Head to head, they try to name the answers most people gave. It is usually where the first proper laugh of the night arrives, at the expense of whoever confidently shouts out the obvious wrong one.

Round 02

The Cube

Composure under a bit of friendly pressure. A run of light challenges that look simple right up until the whole room is watching. Nobody ends up embarrassed; everybody ends up involved.

Round 03

Countdown

The closer. Teams race the clock to build words from a jumble of letters. It is fast, it is tight, and when the scores are close going in, this is the round that decides who walks away with it.

Those are the regulars. We also keep a handful of shorter formats in reserve, including word reveals, scrambles, a spin of the wheel and a few different bingo formats, which we drop in depending on your group and how competitive you want the night to get.

How It Runs

A Big Screen and a Leaderboard Nobody Wants to Lose On

On the night you get a professional host running the show from the front, with a co-host alongside them for larger groups. Guests play in teams. Everything runs off big red buzzers, a big screen and live scoring, so the leaderboard moves in front of everyone and the tension in the room is real rather than polite.

We write the questions around your group. The inside jokes, the director who is famously hopeless at geography, the team that wins the charity bake-off every single year. The more specific it gets, the harder it lands. The whole thing builds to a finale and a clear set of winners, because a competition with no result is just a meeting with buzzers.

Your Setup

The Same Show at Any Size

Smaller Rooms

The Real Buzzers

Licensed gameshow buzzers, the kind you have seen on television. They feel good in the hand and they make a room sound like a studio. We bring the buzzers, the A/V and the screens. You bring the team.

Larger Rooms

The Phone in Your Pocket

Past a certain size, everyone's phone becomes the buzzer instead. No app, no download, just a browser on any device. The setup runs up to 300 devices at once.

You will not have to work out which you need. We look at your numbers and your venue and tell you which setup gives the better night.

In Person · Virtual · Hybrid

Built for a Room, Doesn't Need One

We run the same show fully remote, over video, for teams who are never in the same building, and across the increasingly common middle ground, where half the company is in the office and half is dialled in from kitchens up and down the country.

The people at home are not spectators. They buzz, they score, they win. That matters more than it sounds. Hybrid events usually leave one group feeling like they got the lesser version of the day, and this is one of the few formats where they genuinely do not.

Answers

Common Questions

What is The Gameshow?

The Gameshow is a 90-minute hosted corporate gameshow from On The Buzzer, run across three rounds inspired by Family Fortunes/Family Feud, The Cube and Countdown. Guests play in teams, competing with buzzers, big-screen visuals and live scoring.

Are these the actual television shows?

No. The rounds are On The Buzzer's own creations, inspired by formats like Family Fortunes/Family Feud, The Cube and Countdown, with a modern twist. Guests recognise the formats instantly, so the rules are simple and quick to explain.

How many people can play?

Groups of any size. The browser-based setup supports up to 300 devices at once, and because guests play in teams there is no real limit on total headcount. Smaller groups can use physical gameshow buzzers.

Is there one host or two?

A professional host always runs the show, with a co-host added for larger groups. You never end up with one person managing the microphone and the technology at the same time.

What equipment do you provide?

Everything. Smaller rooms use licensed gameshow buzzers; larger groups use their own phones as buzzers through a browser, with no app to download. Audio-visual kit and screens are included.

Can The Gameshow be run virtually?

Yes. It runs in person, online or hybrid. Remote players buzz in and score live, competing on equal terms with anyone in the room.

What if it falls flat?

It does not, and that is not bravado. The format is the safety net. People compete on instinct, including the ones who warned you beforehand that they "don't really do this sort of thing".

What if it feels cringe?

The quickest way to make an event awkward is to force people to take part. We never do. The host reads the room and adjusts; the energy is professional, not amateur dramatics.

What if the tech fails?

We bring our own kit, set up well before doors, and have run this often enough to have a plan B for the plan B. You are hiring the logistics every bit as much as the show.

What if leadership hates it?

They tend to be the ones who get most competitive. If your culture is more reserved, tell us in advance and we pitch the night accordingly. The format stretches a long way in both directions.
